**The 10 K and 50 K copper foil scans are directly comparable.** They have:

- Identical \(E_0\) at 8977.58 eV
- Nearly identical edge steps: 2.299 vs. 2.290
- Similar energy coverage and usable EXAFS range
- Excellent data quality

**The third scan, labeled 300 K, is not directly comparable without checking or correcting it.** It differs substantially:

- \(E_0 = 8980.50\) eV, about **2.9 eV higher**
- Edge step = 2.73, about **19% larger**
- Shorter post-edge range: 1165 vs. ~2385 eV
- Fewer points and coarser sampling
- Lower, though still adequate, SNR

This pattern is more suggestive of a **different energy calibration, normalization, detector/measurement geometry, or scan setup** than a genuine temperature effect. The 300 K scan is not unusable—it has an excellent quality grade—but I would not compare its raw normalized amplitudes or EXAFS directly with the other two until its energy alignment and normalization are checked. Use a common energy range after alignment, and investigate why its edge step differs.
