**The 10 K and 50 K scans are closely comparable. The 300 K scan needs qualification: it comes from a different experiment and is not a clean third point in a controlled temperature series.**

The source citation identifies **10/50 K as NSLS X11-A measurements from 1992, and room temperature as APS 13ID from 2001**. Thus temperature is confounded with measurement provenance. The roughly 3 eV offset and different absorption scale should not be interpreted as temperature-induced chemistry. Energy registration and normalization are needed before judging the remaining XANES differences.

The 300 K EXAFS amplitude also falls much more rapidly with k: its k²-weighted RMS amplitude relative to 10 K is 0.80 at 3–5 Å⁻¹, 0.46 at 7–9 Å⁻¹, and 0.18 at 13–15 Å⁻¹. This is consistent with stronger thermal Debye–Waller damping, although these separate experiments cannot establish that temperature explains every difference. The 50 K scan shows only modest damping relative to 10 K.

For a subsequent comparison of all three, use consistent processing and a common useful k range, approximately **3–15 Å⁻¹**. The 300 K signal-to-noise ratio is about 6.3 at 13–15 Å⁻¹ but only 1.2 above 15 Å⁻¹. The colder scans also become noise-dominated near 19 Å⁻¹ despite extending to 25 Å⁻¹. Their current transform windows differ, so their existing Fourier-transform amplitudes are not directly comparable.

The “shared reference” is an exact duplicate of the 300 K measurement (`cu_rt01.xmu`), not independent corroboration. All three foils are linked to it; that link does not establish common acquisition or calibration.
