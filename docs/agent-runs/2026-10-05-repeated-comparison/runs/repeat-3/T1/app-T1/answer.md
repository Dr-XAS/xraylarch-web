The **10 K and 50 K copper foil scans are comparable**:

- Same Cu K edge and inferred E0: **8977.58 eV**
- Edge-step ratio: **0.996**
- XANES maximum difference: **0.0086** — very small
- Relative energy shift: **−0.018 eV** — negligible
- Both extend to about **k = 25 Å⁻¹**
- Their χ(k) amplitudes are similar, gradually decreasing for the warmer scan, consistent with modest thermal damping.

The **300 K scan is not directly comparable as currently loaded**:

- Its E0 is **8980.50 eV**, about **2.92 eV** above the 10 K/50 K scans.
- The comparison estimates an energy correction of about **−2.959 eV**.
- Its edge step is substantially larger: **1.187×** the 10 K value.
- XANES difference is **0.3842**, far larger than the 10–50 K difference.
- It contains a much shorter energy range, ending at **10145.86 eV**, and reaches only **k = 17.45 Å⁻¹** rather than 25 Å⁻¹.
- Its χ(k) is much weaker at high k, falling to about **8%** of the 10 K amplitude by the end of its support.

There is also an important data-integrity issue: the 300 K scan and the group labelled **“Cu foil · shared reference”** contain **identical raw arrays** (`cu_rt01.xmu`). Thus the 300 K scan appears to be duplicated as the shared reference, rather than being an independent measurement.

So: **10 K and 50 K form a consistent pair. The 300 K group should not be merged or quantitatively compared directly until its energy calibration and normalization are investigated; even after correction, its truncated range and apparent duplicate/reference status need to be addressed.**
