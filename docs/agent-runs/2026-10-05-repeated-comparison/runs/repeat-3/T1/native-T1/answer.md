All three copper foil scans are **usable and broadly comparable**, but the third scan is not directly interchangeable with the first two without preprocessing.

- **10 K:** excellent quality; \(E_0 \approx 8977.6\) eV, usable to \(k \approx 14.6\ \text{Å}^{-1}\).
- **50 K:** excellent quality and very similar to 10 K; same \(E_0\), comparable edge step and EXAFS range.
- **300 K / room-temperature scan:** still excellent quality, but it differs noticeably:
  - Edge position is shifted by about **+2.9 eV** relative to the two low-temperature scans.
  - Edge step is larger: **2.73 vs. 2.29**, about 19% higher.
  - It ends much earlier in energy, so it has a shorter post-edge/EXAFS region.
  - Its SNR is lower, though still more than adequate.

**Conclusion:** none of the scans is unusable. The **10 K and 50 K scans are directly comparable**. The third scan should first be **energy-aligned and normalized consistently**, and comparisons involving EXAFS should use a common restricted \(k\)-range. The edge-step difference should also be checked for thickness, detector response, or normalization differences rather than interpreted immediately as a temperature effect.
