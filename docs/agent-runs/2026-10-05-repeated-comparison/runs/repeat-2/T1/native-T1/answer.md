All three scans are **fit-worthy**, but they are **not directly comparable without harmonization**.

- **10 K and 50 K:** closely comparable. They have the same \(E_0\) (8977.58 eV), nearly identical energy coverage, edge steps, and usable EXAFS range.
- **300 K:** usable, but less directly comparable:
  - Its edge is shifted by about **+2.9 eV** relative to the other two, suggesting an energy-calibration/alignment offset.
  - It has a substantially shorter high-energy range, ending near **10.15 keV** rather than **11.36 keV**.
  - Its edge step is larger (**2.73 vs. 2.29**), and its SNR is lower, though still good.

So, **none of the scans should be discarded**, but the 300 K scan should be **energy-aligned and comparisons should use their common energy/\(k\)-range**. The larger edge step should also be checked as a possible normalization, thickness, or measurement-mode difference—not automatically interpreted as a temperature effect.
