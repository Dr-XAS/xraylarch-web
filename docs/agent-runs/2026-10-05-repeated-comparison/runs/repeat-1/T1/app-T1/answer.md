The **10 K and 50 K copper foils are comparable**. The **300 K scan is not comparable to them as-is**.

### 10 K vs 50 K
- Same Cu K edge and essentially identical \(E_0\): difference **0.00 eV**
- Edge-step ratio: **0.996** — effectively identical
- Required alignment shift: only **−0.018 eV**
- Maximum XANES difference: **0.0086** — very small
- EXAFS amplitude gradually decreases with temperature, from about **0.997** at \(k=3–5\) to **0.87** at \(k=21–23\), which is consistent with increased thermal disorder at 50 K

These two can reasonably be compared or merged, subject to choosing a common range.

### 300 K
This scan is substantially inconsistent:

- \(E_0\) differs by **2.92 eV**
- It would require an alignment shift of about **−2.959 eV**
- Edge step is **18.7% larger** than the 10 K scan
- Maximum XANES difference: **0.3842**, far larger than the 10/50 K difference
- EXAFS amplitude falls to only **0.084** of the 10 K reference by \(k=13–17.45\)
- It ends much earlier: available \(k_{\max}\) is **17.45**, versus **25** for the 10 K and 50 K scans
- Its raw data are identical to the separate **“Cu foil · shared reference”** group, so this is a duplicate measurement/label rather than an independent scan

The 300 K spectrum may contain genuine thermal damping, but the large energy offset, edge-step discrepancy, XANES mismatch, and severe high-\(k\) loss indicate that it should **not be treated as a directly comparable member of the same dataset without investigating or correcting it**. I would proceed with the 10 K and 50 K scans and keep the 300 K scan separate pending inspection.
